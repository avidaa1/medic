Microbiology

Bacteria

Single-celled prokaryotes. Gram-positive (thick peptidoglycan wall, retains crystal violet stain) vs gram-negative (thin wall, outer membrane, resists stain). Reproduce by binary fission. Major genera: E. coli, Staphylococcus, Streptococcus, Salmonella, Mycobacterium.

bacteriagram-stain

Viruses

Non-cellular; a protein capsid (sometimes lipid envelope) around DNA or RNA. Baltimore classification groups them by genome type and replication strategy. Cannot reproduce outside a host cell.

viruscapsid

Fungi

Eukaryotic; yeasts (unicellular, e.g. Candida) and molds (filamentous). Cause mycoses ranging from superficial (athlete's foot) to systemic (invasive aspergillosis).

fungi

Parasites

Protozoa (single-celled, e.g. Plasmodium, the malaria parasite) and helminths (worms — nematodes, cestodes, trematodes).

parasite

Prions

Misfolded proteins with no genetic material. Template normal proteins into the same misfolded shape. Resist heat, radiation, and standard chemical sterilization because there is no nucleic acid to destroy.

prion

Pathology & Sickness

Infection & Immune Response

Inflammation, fever, and sepsis are the body's escalating responses to a spreading pathogen — sepsis is immune overreaction becoming dangerous in itself.

Flu Types

Influenza A, B, C, D. A causes pandemics (has animal reservoirs, undergoes antigenic shift); B and C are milder, human-adapted; D mainly affects cattle. A subtypes are named by surface proteins: H (hemagglutinin, 18 known) and N (neuraminidase, 11 known) — e.g. H1N1, H3N2, H5N1.

fluh5n1

Common Illnesses

Cold (mostly rhinovirus), flu, pneumonia, gastroenteritis, strep throat — different pathogen classes, different mechanisms, often confused symptomatically.

Chronic Disease

Diabetes, cardiovascular disease, autoimmune conditions — not infections, but long-run failures of metabolic or immune regulation.

Cancer

Not a pathogen — a disease of a cell's own genome escaping control, dividing uncontrollably and evading immune surveillance. Clonal evolution lets it adapt to treatment much like a pathogen adapts to a drug.

Pharmacology & Treatment

Antibiotics

Penicillins, cephalosporins, macrolides, tetracyclines, fluoroquinolones, aminoglycosides. Each targets a bacterial structure or process (cell wall, protein synthesis, DNA replication) absent or different in human cells.

Antivirals

DAAs (hepatitis C), antiretrovirals (HIV), neuraminidase inhibitors (flu), acyclovir-class (herpes) — block a specific step of viral replication rather than killing a cell outright.

Antifungals & Antiparasitics

Azoles, echinocandins, polyenes (fungi); artemisinin-class antimalarials, anthelmintics (parasites).

Antidotes

Naloxone (opioid reversal), flumazenil (benzodiazepine reversal), atropine (organophosphate/nerve agent), N-acetylcysteine (acetaminophen overdose), activated charcoal (GI decontamination), digoxin immune Fab, physostigmine.

Chronic-Disease Drug Classes

Cardiovascular: statins, beta-blockers, ACE inhibitors, anticoagulants. Diabetes: insulin, metformin, GLP-1 agonists. Autoimmune: corticosteroids, immunosuppressants, biologics (TNF inhibitors).

Cancer Treatment

Chemotherapy (alkylating agents, antimetabolites), targeted therapy (kinase inhibitors), immunotherapy (checkpoint inhibitors, CAR-T).

Pain / CNS

NSAIDs, opioids (mechanism and dependence risk, not dosing), anticonvulsants, antidepressants, antipsychotics.

Vaccine Types

Live-attenuated, inactivated, subunit, mRNA, viral vector — different strategies for training immunity without causing the disease.

Immunology

Innate Immunity

Fast, non-specific first response — skin barrier, inflammation, phagocytes (neutrophils, macrophages), the complement system.

Adaptive Immunity

Slower, pathogen-specific, and creates memory. B-cells produce antibodies; T-cells (helper, killer, regulatory) coordinate and directly destroy infected cells.

Regulatory T-cells (Tregs)

Keep the immune system from attacking the body's own tissue. Their discovery (Brunkow, Ramsdell, Sakaguchi) won the 2025 Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine, for work foundational to autoimmune treatment and cancer immunotherapy.

Acquired Immunity

Natural (from infection) vs vaccine-induced — both train the adaptive system, but vaccines do it without the disease risk.

Autoimmunity & Allergy

Autoimmune disease: immune system attacks self tissue. Allergy: immune system overreacts to a harmless substance. Both are regulation failures, not infections.

Signature Section

Why can't we eradicate these?

Three master obstacles show up again and again: latent reservoirs (the pathogen hides, dormant, where drugs can't reach it), rapid mutation or serotype diversity (it changes shape faster than immunity or vaccines can track), and physical indestructibility (prions have no genetic material to attack). Medicine Cabinet

Pain & Fever

Acetaminophen (works centrally, no anti-inflammatory effect) vs NSAIDs like ibuprofen (block prostaglandin synthesis — anti-inflammatory but harder on the stomach and kidneys).

Allergy

Antihistamines block histamine receptors. First-generation (diphenhydramine) cross into the brain and sedate; second-generation (loratadine, cetirizine) mostly don't.

Digestive

Antacids neutralize stomach acid directly; H2 blockers and PPIs reduce acid production upstream — different speeds, different durations.

Storage & Expiration

Heat and light degrade active ingredients over time. An expired medication is usually less potent, not acutely dangerous — but potency loss matters most for anything time-critical (epinephrine, insulin).

Why Some Combinations Matter

Mechanism, not dosing: acetaminophen and alcohol are both processed by the same liver pathway, so combining them concentrates stress on that one pathway rather than splitting the load. This is a biology lesson, not a safety verdict for your situation.
